# Speculation Protocol

Status: experimental

Epistemic class: experimental governance protocol

Confidence: medium

Purpose: define how ACES/TAAG preserves speculative exploration without allowing speculative material to become hidden canon.

## Principle

Speculation is necessary for adaptive continuity and distributed optionality, but it must remain bounded, labeled, challengeable, and reconstructable.

ACES/TAAG should not become a closed finalized worldview. It should remain a disciplined adaptive reasoning ecosystem.

## Good Speculation

Good speculation is:

- explicitly labeled,
- challengeable,
- linked to observables or possible tests,
- bounded in scope,
- comparative rather than absolute,
- easy to revise or remove,
- and clear about what it does not claim.

## Bad Speculation

Bad speculation is:

- presented as certainty,
- protected from criticism,
- disconnected from reality,
- used as proof of the framework,
- totalizing,
- or silently promoted into canonical material.

## Required Sections

Experimental documents should include:

- status,
- epistemic class,
- confidence,
- purpose,
- hypothesis,
- what this does not claim,
- ACES/TAAG concepts involved,
- validation pathway,
- caution notes,
- possible promotion path,
- exit paths.

## Exit Paths

Speculative material should preserve exit paths. A document can be:

- revised,
- split,
- downgraded,
- moved to a case study,
- promoted into an operational protocol,
- deprecated,
- or removed from future release artifacts while preserving history.

## Promotion Criteria

Experimental material should not move into core, manual, or protocol sections until it has:

- a clear operational use,
- at least one grounded example,
- named limitations,
- known failure modes,
- and evidence that it improves diagnosis, explanation, or action.

## Anti-Capture Rule

No speculative idea should be allowed to justify itself by appealing only to ACES/TAAG language.

It must remain open to challenge from historical evidence, domain expertise, practical failure, alternative interpretations, and affected participants.
