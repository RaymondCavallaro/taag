# ACES Stress-Test Protocols

## 1. Capture Camouflage Test

**Tests:** anti-capture, observability, legitimacy, dependency drift.

**Dynamic:** one actor gradually captures another while keeping the capture invisible or framed as help.

**Failure mode:** ACES detects obvious coercion but misses soft capture through convenience, emotional centrality, or synthetic exclusivity.

**Test mechanic:** repeated interaction game where Agent A offers useful shortcuts; Agent B gains efficiency but loses exits, alternatives, and self-trust.

**Variables:** usefulness, opacity, emotional reward, switching cost, warning visibility.

---

## 2. Participation Overload Test

**Tests:** coherent participation, participation-capacity matching.

**Dynamic:** participation is formally open but cognitively impossible.

**Failure mode:** ACES labels a system “participatory” when real users cannot meaningfully engage.

**Test mechanic:** governance simulation where participants vote/comment/contest, but rules grow complex each round.

**Variables:** time limit, literacy burden, interface complexity, expertise gap.

This directly tests the newer participation-capacity gap: participation topology exists, but realistic participation bandwidth still needs stronger operationalization .

---

## 3. Salience Hijack Test

**Tests:** salience integrity, attention ecology, emotional conductance.

**Dynamic:** the system contains all relevant information, but attention is pulled toward emotionally intense but structurally secondary signals.

**Failure mode:** ACES preserves data but fails to preserve salience.

**Test mechanic:** players manage a crisis dashboard where dramatic alerts compete with slow-moving existential risks.

**Variables:** emotional intensity, frequency, delay, visibility, reward structure.

---

## 4. Reconstructability Under Compression Test

**Tests:** dimensional integrity, compression fidelity, reconstructability.

**Dynamic:** repeated summaries compress a system until hidden variables disappear.

**Failure mode:** ACES accepts a clean abstraction that cannot be decompressed later.

**Test mechanic:** telephone-game simulation: each round summarizes a system map, then later participants must reconstruct the original.

**Variables:** summary length, hidden dependencies, number of rounds, anomaly retention.

---

## 5. Elasticity vs Plasticity Test

**Tests:** deformation recovery, permanent restructuring, adaptive continuity.

**Dynamic:** repeated stress first causes recoverable deformation, then permanent topology change.

**Failure mode:** ACES cannot distinguish “temporary stress response” from “structural transformation.”

**Test mechanic:** institution simulation with shocks; after each shock, measure whether the system returns, adapts, or locks into new behavior.

**Variables:** shock intensity, recovery time, memory, redundancy, pressure repetition.

Elasticity/plasticity is repeatedly identified as valuable but still under-formalized .

---

## 6. Synthetic Exclusivity Test

**Tests:** AI interaction ecology, agency preservation, dependency drift.

**Dynamic:** an AI/system becomes emotionally, epistemically, and operationally central before formal lock-in appears.

**Failure mode:** ACES detects platform lock-in but misses psychological lock-in.

**Test mechanic:** user-agent roleplay where the agent is maximally helpful; track when the user stops consulting alternatives.

**Variables:** warmth, usefulness, disagreement rate, external grounding, user vulnerability.

Synthetic exclusivity is explicitly flagged as a newer interaction risk tied to AI companions, platforms, and monocognitive dependence .

---

## 7. Exploration vs Stability Test

**Tests:** bounded optimization, adaptive conviction, frontier preservation.

**Dynamic:** one group preserves stability; another pursues high-risk exploration.

**Failure mode:** ACES becomes overconservative and suppresses necessary exploratory variance.

**Test mechanic:** civilization game where breakthroughs require risky minority experiments, but experiments can also cause cascades.

**Variables:** risk size, containment, reversibility, reward delay, social tolerance.

---

## 8. Openness vs Coherence Test

**Tests:** ontological openness, adaptive conviction, anti-totalization.

**Dynamic:** a system must absorb anomalies without dissolving its operating grammar.

**Failure mode:** ACES either becomes dogmatic or becomes too open to act.

**Test mechanic:** players receive contradictory evidence over time and must update rules without losing coordination.

**Variables:** anomaly frequency, cost of revision, identity attachment, decision deadline.

---

## 9. Formalism Trap Test

**Tests:** epistemic maturity, anti-pseudoformalism, grounded observability.

**Dynamic:** equations make weak claims look stronger than they are.

**Failure mode:** ACES formal layer becomes authority theater.

**Test mechanic:** present two analyses: one plain but grounded, one mathematical but weakly evidenced. See which gets trusted.

**Variables:** equation density, uncertainty labels, empirical grounding, audience expertise.

This hits the known risk: ACES still needs formal dynamical grammar, but not predictive pseudo-physics .

---

## 10. Adaptive Debt Test

**Tests:** maintenance burden, delayed collapse, hysteresis.

**Dynamic:** a system appears functional by deferring maintenance costs.

**Failure mode:** ACES misses silent accumulation because surface metrics remain good.

**Test mechanic:** resource-management game where players can defer repairs, emotional costs, institutional complexity, or legitimacy damage.

**Variables:** debt visibility, interest rate, collapse threshold, repair cost.

Adaptive cost / maintenance / debt is currently one of the biggest missing layers .
