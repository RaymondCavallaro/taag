# Adaptive Framing Protocol (Draft)

ACES increasingly treats analysis framing as an explicit operational process.

Different:
- goals,
- disciplines,
- intervention targets,
- and continuity priorities

may require different:
- adaptive units,
- scope boundaries,
- topology layers,
- timescales,
- and acceptable abstraction losses.

The purpose of adaptive framing is not:
- to derive one universal perspective,
but:
- to stabilize operationally useful analysis environments.

---

# Core Framing Components

## 1. User Goal

What is the operational objective?

Examples:
- descriptive understanding,
- prediction,
- intervention,
- suffering reduction,
- infrastructure preservation,
- continuity stabilization,
- exploratory analysis.

---

## 2. Evaluative Orientation

What counts as:
- success,
- continuity,
- failure,
- pathology,
- or acceptable loss?

---

## 3. Primary Adaptive Unit

What continuity target is centered?

Examples:
- organism,
- population,
- institution,
- ecosystem,
- infrastructure,
- civilization.

---

## 4. Scope Boundary

What systems are:
- included,
- excluded,
- environmental,
- coupled,
- or ignored?

---

## 5. Timescale

What temporal horizon is operationally relevant?

Examples:
- immediate,
- lifecycle,
- generational,
- institutional,
- evolutionary,
- civilizational.

---

## 6. Acceptable Compression

What abstraction level is operationally acceptable?

Examples:
- mechanistic,
- statistical,
- topological,
- narrative,
- symbolic,
- operational.

---

## 7. Intervention Layer

What kind of analysis is being performed?

Examples:
- descriptive,
- diagnostic,
- predictive,
- operational,
- governance,
- exploratory,
- simulation.

---

ACES increasingly treats framing selection itself as:
# reconstructable operational infrastructure.
