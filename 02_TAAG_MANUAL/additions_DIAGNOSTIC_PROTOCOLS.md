# Technical Framing Diagnostic

## Purpose

Detect whether a system uses:

# technical legitimacy projection
without preserving reconstructable diagnostics.

to preserve:
- bounded inference,
- reconstructability,
- interpretive stability,
- and topology-sensitive analysis.

ACES preserves:
- actor-level specificity,
- reconstructable evidence,
- topology-sensitive interpretation,
- and bounded inference

while avoiding:
- demographic collapse,
- collective attribution,
- identity essentialization,
- and dehumanizing generalization.

---

## Diagnostic Questions

* Which variables are optimized?
* Which variables are excluded?
* Which continuity layers remain unmeasured?
* Which assumptions are hidden?
* Which tradeoffs are externalized?
* Which topology conditions are omitted?
* Which uncertainties remain invisible?
* Which proxy variables replaced direct observability?
* Which interpretations exceed available evidence?
* Which optimization pressures distort viability?

---

## Important Distinction

A system may remain:

* technically sophisticated,
* quantitatively grounded,
* operationally effective,
* and statistically coherent

while still degrading:

* adaptive continuity,
* participation integrity,
* future-space,
* reconstructability,
* or long-horizon viability.

Optimization therefore should NOT be confused with:

# adaptive health.

ACES analyzes:

- actors,
- institutions,
- incentives,
- behaviors,
- operational structures,
- topology conditions,
- coordination systems,
- and reconstructable evidence

without collapsing analysis into:

- demographic blame,
- collective guilt,
- inherited responsibility,
- or identity-essentialized interpretation.

High-sensitivity analysis domains require:

- explicit uncertainty visibility,
- actor-level specificity,
- contextualization,
- reconstructable evidence,
- and anti-essentialization safeguards.

ACES therefore reinforces:

"analysis of a specific actor or institution
does NOT imply judgment of any broader population
the actor may belong to."

ACES rejects:

- collective guilt attribution,
- inherited moral responsibility,
- demographic essentialization,
- or topology-insensitive group generalization.

Observed behaviors by individuals,
institutions,
or networks
should NOT be automatically generalized toward broader populations.

Observed correlation alone should NOT be treated as:

- complete causal explanation,
- immutable identity property,
- moral classification,
- or deterministic future prediction.

ACES preserves:
- grounded observability,
- actor-level specificity,
- reconstructable evidence,
- uncertainty visibility,
- and topology-sensitive interpretation

while resisting:
- narrative collapse,
- demographic essentialization,
- collective attribution,
- dehumanization,
- and coercive operationalization.
